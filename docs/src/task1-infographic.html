Parents aren’t anti-gaming.
They’re anxious.

What 67 Indian parents whose children play online games told us about benefits, fears, rules and family life.

1Mixed feelings

Most see a distraction. Half also see a stress-buster.

Gaming can be a distraction58/67 86.6%
Gaming is a good stress-buster37/67 55.2%
Hold both views at once35/67 52.2%
2What they fear most

Addiction and grades, more than violence.

Addiction38/67 56.7%
Academic performance35/67 52.2%
Health effects28/67 41.8%
Aggressive behaviour21/67 31.3%
Share of parents reporting high fear.
3Fear & family

Fear travels with family conflict.

63.2%
report medium/high conflict when addiction fear is high (24/38)
44.8%
when it isn’t high (13/29)
Overall fear vs family impact: Spearman ρ = 0.57. An association, not proof of cause.
4Rules aren’t the fix

97% say rules are needed. Rules don’t mean calmer homes.

57.4%
medium/high conflict with rules (31/54)
46.2%
with no rules (6/13, small group)
No meaningful difference (Fisher p = 0.54).
5Understanding helps

Parents who understand why their child plays are half as likely to be highly fearful.

High fear = average across 5 fear items at or above midway between ‘medium’ and ‘high’. Mann-Whitney p = 0.037.
Don’t understand why8/14 57.1%
Understand why15/53 28.3%
Takeaway

Curiosity before control. Ask what your child enjoys, play once together, then agree rules with them, not for them.

Source: YourDOST parent survey (Google Form), 100 responses collected 1–3 Dec 2020. Analysed n = 67 parents whose children play online games (33 ‘No’ respondents skipped all questions by design). Convenience sample during COVID lockdowns; findings are associations, not causes. Full method: task1-gaming-analysis.pdf.
