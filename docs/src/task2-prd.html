Smart therapist matching

YourDOST Match. Find the right person to talk to.

The best therapist isn’t the most popular one. It’s the one who fits the person’s needs, preferences, availability and continuity. This PRD defines a hybrid, explainable matching system that replaces browse-and-filter with three well-reasoned suggestions, while always leaving the choice with the user.

North Star

Successful first-session-to-follow-up continuity rate

Users who complete a first session and book a second with the same therapist within 21 days.

MVP bet

A 6-question intake + 3 explained matches

A rules-plus-weights engine, no black-box ML until real outcome data exists.

Status

Prototype implemented

The engine runs live in the Wellbeing Compass prototype (Task 3): lib/matching.ts.

1Problem statement

Today users browse a directory, apply filters and choose a therapist on their own. That works for people who already know what counselling is and what they need. It fails the majority of first-timers in a B2B2C context: employees and students who arrive uncertain, often at a hard moment, and are asked to make an expert judgement (which specialisation? which approach?) before they’ve said a word to anyone. The result is decision friction: drop-off before booking, poor-fit first sessions, and users who never come back for a second session even though continuity is where counselling value compounds.

2Core problems

Decision paralysis

Many similar-looking profiles and no guidance on how to choose; users defer the decision indefinitely.

Specialisation jargon

‘CBT’, ‘ACT’ and ‘systemic’ mean little to a first-timer; users can’t map their concern to expertise.

Expectation mismatch

A user needing sleep and burnout support books a relationship specialist because they were first or most-reviewed.

Scheduling friction

The ‘right’ therapist has no slot for 10 days; the user discovers this only after reading profiles.

Lack of continuity

Nothing nudges a second session with the same person; users re-browse, restart their story, or lapse.

Abandonment before booking

Each extra decision (filter, profile, slot) is a drop-off point, and emotional effort is already high.

Problem framing is based on the assignment brief and common patterns in digital counselling funnels. YourDOST’s actual funnel baselines were not available; all targets below are assumptions to be replaced with measured baselines in week 1.

Intake design principles

  • One question per screen, all optional except concern; ‘Prefer not to say’ everywhere.
  • Plain language (“What would you like help with?”), never clinical labels.
  • Prefill from the employer profile only where the user consents (e.g. language).
  • Show the privacy promise before the first question.

Match screen principles

  • Exactly three suggestions: enough to feel chosen, few enough to decide.
  • Reasons are tied to the user’s own answers (“Speaks Bengali”, “Evening slots this week”).
  • No star ratings or popularity ranking as the lead signal.
  • ‘None of these feel right’ leads to rematch or full directory, with no penalty.

How the weights get calibrated

  1. Clinician review (pre-launch): 3–5 YourDOST clinical leads rank 30 anonymised intake → shortlist cases; adjust weights until engine-vs-clinician top-3 agreement is at least 70%.
  2. Shadow mode (weeks 1–4): log engine top-3 next to what users actually pick in the existing directory; no user impact.
  3. Outcome labels: a ‘good match’ = felt understood (yes) and booked a 2nd session with the same therapist within 21 days.
  1. Fit weights to outcomes: a logistic regression of good-match on the six factor scores; use its coefficients to inform (not replace) weights. Re-fit quarterly.
  2. A/B test weight variants on the North Star with guardrails; clinicians sign off on any change over ±5 points.
  3. Fairness audits: check score distributions by therapist gender, language and tenure so new or minority-language therapists aren’t systematically buried.

YourDOST · Task 2Matching over time & requirements

First-time and continuous matching

First session

  • Short intake (6 questions, ~60s).
  • Exactly 3 recommendations, each with reasons and next slot.
  • ‘See more’ and ‘browse all’ always visible.
  • Never auto-assign; booking is always the user’s tap.

After the first session

  • Optional 3-tap check-in: Were you comfortable? Did you feel understood? Continue with them?
  • ‘Yes’ to continue pins the therapist and offers their next 3 slots.
  • ‘Not really’ triggers a no-blame rematch excluding that therapist.

Over time

  • Prioritise continuity (+15 boost, pinned card).
  • Learn from explicit feedback only.
  • Rematch available any time, from any screen.
  • Never treat low engagement as a sign of poor mental health; silence just means no nudge beyond one gentle reminder.

9MVP scope (6–8 weeks)

  • FR-1 to FR-7, FR-9, FR-10; rule + weight engine (no ML).
  • Feedback-informed preference score (with minimum-volume threshold).
  • Continuity pin and simple rematch.
  • Shadow-mode logging, then a 50/50 A/B test vs. the current directory.
  • Web and app, English + Hindi intake copy.

10Out of scope (v1)

  • ML / collaborative-filtering recommender (needs outcome data).
  • Free-text NLP of user concerns beyond keyword safety screening.
  • Clinical assessments or severity scoring inside matching.
  • Dynamic pricing or paid-tier steering.
  • Group therapy and psychiatrist matching.
  • Therapist-side ranking dashboards (risk of gaming the metric).

YourDOST · Task 2Risk, safety & success

11Edge cases

CaseBehaviour
No therapist passes Stage 1Explain which filter excluded everyone; offer to relax it (e.g. language → ‘preferred’); always show human-support contact.
Only 1–2 eligibleShow what exists honestly; never pad with ineligible profiles.
User skips everything except concernNeutral scores for unknown preferences; reasons rely on specialisation and availability.
‘Something else’ concernGeneralists ranked first; offer a 15-min intake call with a counsellor.
Preferred therapist leaves or is fullNotify, offer the closest match by factor profile, and carry intake answers over.
Negative feedback after session 1No-blame rematch; exclude that therapist; feedback is not shown to the therapist individually.
Crisis language in intakeMatching stops; crisis panel (112, Tele-MANAS 14416) and the human support team. Ordinary sadness or stress is not treated as crisis.
Minors (students under 18)Route to therapists approved for minors; follow institutional consent policy.
Cold start for new therapistsFeedback factor uses the platform mean until 20 responses; capped exploration slots in the top 3.

12Privacy & safety

  • Employer never sees individual intake, matches or bookings; only aggregated, k-anonymised (k ≥ 10) usage.
  • Intake stored separately from identity, encrypted at rest; free text not retained after matching.
  • Matching is a navigation aid, not a clinical decision; clinicians can override and audit.
  • No diagnosis labels or severity scores anywhere in the flow.
  • Explicit consent for using feedback to improve matches; user can delete history.
  • Compliant with India’s DPDP Act 2023 principles: purpose limitation, minimisation, consent.

Rollout

  • Wk 0–2: baselines, clinician calibration, copy testing with 8–10 users.
  • Wk 3–4: shadow mode.
  • Wk 5–8: A/B test, 2 pilot corporate clients, 50% of first-time users.
  • Decision rule: ship if continuity improves with no guardrail breach; otherwise iterate on weights and copy.

Engagement metrics show that matching makes good support easier to reach. They are not evidence that people are getting better. Clinical outcomes are tracked separately, by clinicians, with validated instruments, and are never optimised directly by the ranking.
